---
title: Joining Bonus
pov: Ananya
date: Thursday, 19 February – Friday, 20 February 2026
place: Northstar Institute of Technology, Indore
---

Nobody her age watches the news. The news finds you anyway, already chewed.

Ananya Rao lies across her bed with her legs straight up the wall and her phone held over her face, and by half past one in the morning the meteorite has been through the internet's entire digestive system.

A streamer edit: the anchor's clip slowed down, bass-boosted, red zoom lines closing in on the impact pit, a caption screaming *BRO IT'S OVER 💀*.

Swipe.

A gaming streamer clutching his head at his desk. *GUYS. GUYS. ALIENS. I CALLED IT IN 2019.*

Swipe.

The drone footage recut to movie-trailer music, a fake countdown, a title card: *EARTH — FINAL SEASON.*

Swipe.

One line of white caption burned over the impact footage: *aliens really came before GTA 6 💀*

Swipe.

A dog staring at the sky. Four hundred thousand likes.

Her hair is loose, which it only ever is in this room. She is wearing one of her nine black T-shirts. Beside her on the pillow there is a tall can of Pringles, half gone, and a bar of dark chocolate, three-quarters gone, and she has decided not to think about either number.

The room is four years of her. A black belt hangs off the cupboard handle, earned in school, real karate, the kind of thing she never mentions and never forgets. A stack of books she actually reads — philosophy, a fat one on personal finance, a novel with a broken spine — and not one of them on the syllabus. Clothes over the chair. A cheap desk lamp. Her campus ID card. And taped to the wall above the desk, printed in unnecessarily expensive colour ink:

> **NEXORA TECHNOLOGIES — PRE-PLACEMENT OFFER**

She looks at it. Then back at the phone, where the anchor is being bass-boosted into oblivion. Then at the offer again.

If aliens have come to destroy Earth, she thinks, they could have waited until after her joining bonus.

She locks the phone. It buzzes immediately.

> **SID:** BRO.
> **SID:** METEORITE.
> **SID:** DID YOU SEE?

She types with one thumb.

> **ANANYA:** YES.

Three dots.

> **SID:** IS THIS HOW IT STARTS?

She smiles at the ceiling.

> **ANANYA:** NO.
> **ANANYA:** IT STARTS WITH YOU FAILING YOUR INTERNSHIP.

> **SID:** BLOCKED.

She drops the phone on her stomach and looks at the offer letter for a while. A salary that, six months ago, would have sounded like fiction. Home is Goa, and these four years are the longest the sea has ever been out of walking distance. In June she starts at Nexora, and the plan after that is very simple. Money first. Then everything else.

"Nexora," she says to the ceiling, the way you say the name of a place you are already living in.

The phone lights up again.

> **INSHORTS:** International scientists request access to meteorite impact site

She ignores it.

> **INSHORTS:** No radiation detected at impact site, say Canadian authorities

She ignores that too, and opens Instagram, where life is normal. Campus memes. Placement memes. Two people from her batch arguing about politics in a comment thread that has gone on for three days. Valorant clips. A plate of poha. Someone's engagement. Someone's breakup. A reel of a professor falling asleep in his own lecture.

The world is fine. The world is extremely fine.

* * *

In the morning, Northstar Institute of Technology does what it does every weekday: pretends to be a university while functioning as a small, sleep-deprived city.

The campus sprawls across the edge of Indore, all red brick and young neem trees and hostels named after scientists nobody has read. At nine it is still cool enough for hoodies. Bikes, coffee, backpacks, people running late, people running later. The unmistakable chaos of a place that has convinced several thousand twenty-year-olds that they are simultaneously geniuses and completely unemployable.

A banner stretches between two lamp posts:

> **NORTHSTAR TECHNICAL FEST — 2026**

And under it, in smaller and sadder letters:

> **FINAL-YEAR STUDENTS: PLACEMENT EXIT SURVEY**

Ananya walks through the crowd with one earphone in, coffee in one hand, phone in the other, hair pulled back in its usual ponytail. A group of juniors parts for her the way juniors do for anyone in their final semester. One of them gathers his courage.

"*Didi.*" [Sis.]

She takes out the earphone. "Hmm?"

"*Placement ho gaya na?*" [You got placed, right?]

She considers him. "No."

He looks confused.

"They accidentally hired me," she says, and puts the earphone back in.

"Ananya!"

She keeps walking.

"ANANYA!"

Sid Laishram arrives at a run, out of breath, backpack bouncing. He is from Imphal, and he has the kind of Northeast-Indian face that strangers on the mainland keep filing under *Chinese? Korean?* He stopped correcting them years ago. It saves time, he says, and they never listen anyway.

"You walk like you're already late for your first day at Nexora," he says.

"I walk like I don't want to talk to you."

He grins. "Same company."

"Unfortunately."

They fall into step. Sid is the only person she knows who can be cheerful before ten.

"Did you see the meteorite thing?"

"Everyone saw it."

"Space dust," Sid says.

"Meteorite."

"Space dust inside meteorite."

"You're making it sound worse."

"It's actually kind of cool," he says. "Something from space just — lands. In a forest. And nobody saw it coming. All those telescopes and nobody saw it coming."

"You have an internship and a PPO," she says. "Your standards for entertainment are gone."

Outside the student centre, a cluster of second-years is gathered under the wall-mounted television. A Hindi news channel is running the meteorite story with the same drone shot, now with dramatic music and a red BREAKING strip that has been on the screen so long it has stopped meaning anything. The morning anchor is calm, almost bored.

"Authorities have confirmed that the research team sent to the impact site found no evidence of radioactive contamination. Officials continue to describe the event as —"

One of the second-years picks up the remote and flips to the cricket.

Nobody objects. Ananya notices it the way you notice a door closing in another room. Not for her. Nothing to do with her. The news gone in a single click.

In the half-second before it changes, a line of text is crawling along the bottom of the screen. She only catches part of it.

> …TEMPORARILY SUSPEND ACCESS TO SURROUNDING FOREST REGI…

Then it's a fast bowler walking back to his mark, and she and Sid are past.

* * *

The equation on the board has been growing for twenty minutes and has developed opinions.

Ananya is looking at her laptop, where a spreadsheet is open:

> **POST-GRADUATION FINANCES**

Salary. Rent. Parents. Travel. Emergency fund. She adds a row.

> **New laptop**

She considers it. Deletes it. Types:

> **MacBook Pro M5**

And then, after a moment of honest reflection:

> **Dyson headphones**

Beside her, Aditya Kulkarni is taking notes in a handwriting so neat it looks typeset. He is twenty, composed, and the kind of studious that makes professors nervous because he might actually ask a question. He doesn't look up.

"You're supposed to be taking notes."

"I'm planning my financial future."

"You have three months left."

"Exactly."

Now he looks, just his eyes sliding sideways to her screen.

"You allocated thirty thousand a month for restaurants."

"Inflation."

"You don't eat at restaurants."

"Future me does."

The professor turns from the board.

"Ms. Rao."

Ananya closes the spreadsheet with a speed that would have impressed her karate sensei.

"Sir?"

"Would you like to explain the solution?"

She looks at the board. The board looks back.

"What," she says, "was the question?"

A few people laugh. The professor stares at her. She stares at him, completely unbothered, and more people laugh, and eventually he turns back to the equation with the expression of a man who has made his peace with a great many things.

Ananya smiles to herself.

This is her world. She understands its rules: what matters, what doesn't, how much you can get away with and for how long. Three more months of it and then the next world, the one with a salary in it.

She belongs here. It is the most comfortable she has felt all week.
