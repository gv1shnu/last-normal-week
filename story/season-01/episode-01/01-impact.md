---
title: Impact
pov: none
date: Thursday, 19 February 2026
place: Northern Ontario, Canada
---

Boreal forest under a grey winter sky. Black spruce, jack pine, snow to the knee. No roads. No buildings. No people for forty kilometres in any direction.

High above, a point of light draws a thin white line across the cloud.

It is bright enough to see in daylight, and it burns a hard, wrong shade of white.

It breaks apart as it falls. Pieces flare and fade like sparks off a grinder. Then, high above the treeline, the light goes out.

For a long time there is nothing. Somewhere far off, a dull double boom rolls across the forest, and a raven lifts off a branch and complains about it.

Then a single fragment, no bigger than a fist, comes down through the trees.

It clips a spruce on the way. A dull crack. A spray of snow and needles. No shockwave. No fire.

The stone sits at the bottom of a shallow pit, no wider than a dinner plate, at the base of the tree. Its crust is black and glassy, like something glazed in a kiln. The snow around it does not melt. Frost is already forming on its surface, a fine white fur across the black.

For several seconds, everything is silent.

Where the crust has split, a thin, almost invisible mist seeps out.

It lies low for a moment, heavier than the air around it. Then it drifts between the trees.

The wind takes it.

* * *

Two hours later, the story is the forty-third item on a polished international news bulletin, between a currency summit and a panda.

> **UNUSUAL METEORITE IMPACT RECORDED IN NORTHERN CANADA**

"Scientists are calling today's meteorite impact unusual," the anchor says, "because of the chemical composition detected around the impact site."

Behind her, a drone shot: snow, trees, a dark dent at the foot of a spruce, and three figures in orange jackets standing well back from it.

"The object entered Earth's atmosphere shortly after eleven a.m. local time. Its fireball was recorded by weather satellites and by dashboard cameras as far away as Sudbury, and the fragment came down in a remote forest region approximately —"

Nobody who matters to this story is watching.
