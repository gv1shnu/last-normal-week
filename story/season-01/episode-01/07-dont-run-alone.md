---
title: Don't Run Alone
pov: Kabir
date: Wednesday, 25 February 2026
place: Northstar Institute of Technology, Indore
---

No game today. Nobody felt like it, not even Kabir, which is how he knows it's serious.

He's pacing the side of the outdoor badminton court with his phone to his ear, and his mother is talking in the fast, low Hindi she uses when she's frightened and doesn't want him to know.

"*Beta, ghar aa ja. Papa gaadi le ke aa jayenge, aadhe ghante mein.*" [Son, come home. Papa will bring the car, half an hour.]

"Mummy, they said stay on campus. It's on the notice board. District administration and all."

"*Notice board se kya hota hai?*" [What does a notice board know?]

Home is twenty-two kilometres away, on the other side of Indore. Kabir has done it on a scooter in thirty-five minutes. He could be eating his mother's aloo paratha by one o'clock. The thought sits in his stomach like a hand pulling.

Arjun is sitting on the bench, not pacing, not on his phone. Arjun is watching the main gate.

"They're locking the gate," Arjun says.

"They lock it every night," Kabir says, half listening.

"It's noon."

Kabir stops pacing. Looks.

The two security guards at the main gate are swinging it shut. One of them loops a heavy chain through the bars, twice, and snaps a padlock on it. The other one pulls on the gate to test it, the way you test a thing you don't trust.

Kabir has been at Northstar for nearly three years. He has never seen that gate closed in daylight.

"Mummy," he says, "I'll call you back."

"Kabir —"

He lowers the phone. "Okay," he says to Arjun. "That's a little —"

A scream.

Not close. From the far end of campus, from the direction of the boys' hostels. And not a scream of pain. Kabir knows what pain sounds like; he's twisted an ankle in front of a hundred people. This is the scream of somebody seeing something.

Every head on the lawn turns toward it. For one long second, nobody moves.

Then Kabir is running toward it, because that's what his legs do before his head gets a vote.

"Kabir!" Arjun shouts, and then, because he's Arjun, he runs after him.

* * *

D-block is chaos in a narrow space.

Students are pouring out of their rooms into the corridor, half dressed, some still holding toothbrushes. A boy Kabir knows by face is pressed flat against the wall, pointing down the corridor with a shaking arm, trying to say something and producing only breath.

At the far end of the corridor, by the bathrooms, someone is standing.

It takes Kabir a moment to recognise him, because he's standing wrong. Head tilted. Barefoot on the tiles. A dark stain spreading down one sleeve of his hoodie from a bandage on the wrist.

Rohit. Third year, mechanical. Plays terrible guitar at two in the morning. He got back from Delhi yesterday, feverish, with his wrist wrapped up — a stray dog at the airport parking, he said — and everybody blamed the journey. Kabir himself said *Delhi ki hawa, bhai* [It's the Delhi air, bro] and handed him a strip of paracetamol.

Rohit isn't looking at them.

"He's not looking at us," Arjun says, very quietly, right behind Kabir's shoulder. "He's listening."

Rohit's mouth opens.

The sound that comes out is low and wet and almost musical.

*HNNNNG…*

Every hair on Kabir's arms stands up at once. It isn't a word. It isn't a scream. It's something he has never heard come out of a person, and his whole body understands it before his brain does: it's a call.

The corridor empties in both directions.

* * *

They hit the mess hall at a dead run, against the flow — everyone else is pouring out, chairs scraping, trays clattering, the doorway jammed with bodies. The rumour got here before they did, and now it's a stampede.

Kabir shoulders through. Arjun follows in his wake.

At a table near the back, three people are on their feet. The tall girl from the badminton hall — ma'am — with her hand already gripping the sleeve of a guy who looks like he's trying to make a joke and can't find one. A third, serious guy hugging a laptop to his chest.

"Don't go toward D-block," Kabir says, breathless. "Do *not* go toward D-block."

The girl looks at him. At Arjun. Back at him. There's recognition there, faint and unenthusiastic.

"Who are you?"

"Not important right now," Kabir says, "ma'am."

Her eyes narrow, and for some reason that's the most reassuring thing that has happened all day.

A crash from the corridor outside. Close. Glass, and something heavy going over. All five of them flinch.

The guy with the laptop speaks. His voice is the only calm one in the room, quiet and fast.

"We need a door that locks and a room with one entrance. Does anyone know one?"

Silence. Kabir's mind is a blank white screen. He's lived here three years and he can't think of a single room.

Then Arjun, hesitant, the way he says everything:

"The library basement. The archive room. One door. It bolts from inside."

They all look at him.

Arjun shrugs, embarrassed. "I hide there before exams."

The girl makes a decision. Kabir watches it happen — a second where she weighs it, and then she's decided, and it's done. He knows that look. It's the look of the one player in a lobby of strangers who just starts calling the play, and everybody follows because somebody has to.

"Then we go to the library," she says.

She looks at the four of them, one by one. Kabir can practically see her sorting them. The joker. The nerd with the laptop. The scared quiet kid who knows where to hide. And him — the jock, he supposes. Fair.

"Stay together," she says. "Don't run alone."

"Why not alone?" asks the joker.

She hesitates, just for a moment, and Kabir sees her remember something — something she saw on a screen and didn't want to understand.

"Because whatever this is," she says, "it doesn't."

She pushes toward the door. The others follow. Kabir's phone buzzes in his pocket, and buzzes again, and he knows without looking that it's his mother, and that the car is coming, and that he isn't going to be in it.

He follows her out.

The last normal week is over.
